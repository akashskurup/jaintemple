The Parva Calendar

The Jain year does not run on the Gregorian calendar. It runs on tithis — so each parva falls on a fixed lunar day and a different English date every year.

How to read this page. Every entry below gives the tithi — the lunar date — which is what the Sangh actually works from and which never changes. The corresponding English dates shift each year, and the derasar’s own programme, timings and labharthi lists are announced on the compound-wall banners a few weeks in advance. For the current year’s dates, ring 099674 76524.

    Paryushan, the eight days

    If you visit a derasar once a year, this is the week. Pravachan every day, the Kalpasutra read through, the Mahavir-janma-vachan on the fifth day, tapasya across the whole Sangh, and pratikraman each evening.

    It closes on Samvatsari with the Barsa-sutra and the great pratikraman — and then everyone, without exception, asks everyone else for forgiveness. મિચ્છામિ દુક્કડમ્.

    Chaturmas

    From Ashadh Sud 14 to Kartik Purnima, sadhus and sadhvis stop travelling and stay in one place for the four months of rain — so as not to harm the countless small lives that come out in the wet. It is the season of the most pravachan and the most tapasya, and it is why Kartik Purnima feels like a release.

    Every fortnight

    Bij, Pancham, Agiyaras and Chaudas — the second, fifth, eleventh and fourteenth of each lunar fortnight — are the recurring parva tithis. Many in the Sangh keep upvas or ayambil on them, and the derasar is noticeably fuller.

    Beyond the calendar

    What else happens here through the year

    Pratishtha Mahotsav anniversaries

    The derasar marks the anniversary of its jin-bimb pratishtha — the installation of the idols — which is recorded on a marble plaque above the gabhara. The mahotsav that accompanies it is announced at the gate.

    Attham & tapasya mahotsav

    Three-day mahotsavs built around a collective attham tap, with shakrastav abhishek at dawn, akhand japa through the day, the ashtaprakari puja, an acharya’s pravachan and sandhya bhakti in the evening. Posh Dashami is the classic occasion.

    Pathshala & shibir

    Children’s pathshala in the hall behind the derasar, and the shibirs — short residential or day camps in the vacations, where sutras, stavans and the puja vidhi are taught properly rather than by imitation.

    Anukampa camps

    Medical camps and medicine assistance run through the Jain Anukampa Group, Karelibaug, whose notices hang on the same railings as the mahotsav banners.

    Tirth-yatra

    After Kartik Purnima the yatra season opens and sanghs set out — Shatrunjay, Girnar, Shankheshwar, Sammet Shikhar. The three tirth-patas on the hall wall are of exactly these places.

    Sandhya bhakti & bhavna

    Evenings of collective singing after the aarti on parva days — stavans and bhavna, sometimes until quite late. The lamps along the compound wall are lit for these.

    Want the exact dates for this year?

    Tithi-to-date conversion changes annually, and the Sangh’s own programme is set locally. A phone call is the reliable route — and if you would like the current year’s calendar published here, send it in and it will be.
