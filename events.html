The Parva Calendar

The Jain year runs on tithis, not on the Gregorian calendar — so each parva falls on a fixed lunar day, and a different English date every year.

    Paryushan, the eight days

    If you visit a derasar once a year, this is the week. Pravachan every day, the Kalpasutra read through, the Mahavir-janma-vachan on the fifth day, tapasya across the whole Sangh, and pratikraman each evening.

    It closes on Samvatsari with the Barsa-sutra and the great pratikraman — and then everyone, without exception, asks everyone else for forgiveness. મિચ્છામિ દુક્કડમ્.

    Chaturmas

    From Ashadh Sud 14 to Kartik Purnima, sadhus and sadhvis stop travelling and stay in one place through the four months of rain — so as not to harm the countless small lives that come out in the wet. It is the season of the most pravachan and the most tapasya, and it is why Kartik Purnima feels like a release.

    Every fortnight

    Bij, Pancham, Agiyaras and Chaudas — the second, fifth, eleventh and fourteenth of each lunar fortnight — are the recurring parva tithis. Many in the Sangh keep upvas or ayambil on them, and the derasar is noticeably fuller.

    This year’s dates

    Each entry alongside gives the tithi, which never changes. The Sangh’s own programme, timings and labharthi lists go up on the compound-wall banners a few weeks ahead — or ring 099674 76524.

    Beyond the calendar

    What else happens here through the year

    Pratishtha Mahotsav anniversaries

    The derasar marks the anniversary of its jin-bimb pratishtha — the installation of the idols — recorded on a marble plaque above the gabhara. The mahotsav that accompanies it is announced at the gate.

    Attham & tapasya mahotsav

    Three-day mahotsavs around a collective attham tap: shakrastav abhishek at dawn, akhand japa through the day, the ashtaprakari puja, an acharya’s pravachan, and sandhya bhakti in the evening.

    Pathshala & shibir

    Children’s pathshala in the hall behind the derasar, and the shibirs in the vacations, where sutras, stavans and the puja vidhi are taught properly rather than by imitation.

    Anukampa camps

    Medical camps and medicine assistance run through the Jain Anukampa Group, Karelibaug, whose notices hang on the same railings as the mahotsav banners.

    Tirth-yatra

    After Kartik Purnima the yatra season opens and sanghs set out — Shatrunjay, Girnar, Shankheshwar, Sammet Shikhar. The three tirth-patas hanging in this derasar’s hall are of exactly these places.

    Planning around a parva day?

    Tithi-to-date conversion changes annually and the Sangh sets its own programme locally. One call gives you the times, the vidhi and whether a mahotsav is running.
