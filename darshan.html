Darshan, Puja & Vidhi

When the doors open, what happens inside them, and the small courtesies that make a derasar a derasar.

Daily schedule

The day in the derasar

All times are Indian Standard Time. The row that lights up is whatever comes next, right now.

These times are indicative, not official. This derasar publishes no hours on its Google listing. The schedule follows the customary Shwetambar Murtipujak routine, which is close but never exact — and on parva days it changes entirely. One phone call to 099674 76524 will give you the real thing.

Why the sanctum rests at midday

After the madhyahn puja the gabhara is closed and the Bhagwan is left undisturbed through the afternoon. It is not a lunch break for the staff — it is part of the vidhi, and it is why an unplanned 2 p.m. visit usually means darshan from the mandap only.

The best hour to come

Early morning, while the prakshal and puja are being done and the derasar is still cool and nearly empty — or the evening aarti, when the lamps are lit and the whole hall sings the Mangal Divo together. Either is worth arranging your day around.

The vidhi

What is actually performed

Prakshal & Abhishek

પ્રક્ષાલ અને અભિષેક

The first act of the morning. The previous day’s offerings are removed and the idol is bathed — pure water, then the panchamrut where the vidhi calls for it — before being wiped dry with soft cloth by hand. Only then is anything else offered.

Ashtaprakari Puja

અષ્ટપ્રકારી પૂજા

The eight-fold offering, in order: jal (water), chandan (sandal), pushpa (flower), dhoop (incense), deep (lamp), akshat (rice), naivedya (sweet) and phal (fruit). Each carries its own meaning — the lamp for kevalgnan, the rice for the state beyond rebirth.

Snatra Puja

સ્નાત્ર પૂજા

The ceremonial abhishek sung by the assembled Sangh, re-enacting the bathing of the newborn Tirthankara by the Indras on Meru. Usually sponsored — for a birthday, a tithi, a family remembrance — and performed with the whole hall joining the stavans.

Chaityavandan

ચૈત્યવંદન

The seated vandana done facing the Bhagwan — sutras, stavan and the stuti, performed on the mandap floor rather than at the idol. This is what most visitors do, and it needs nothing but a clean cloth to sit on and a few quiet minutes.

Aarti & Mangal Divo

આરતી અને મંગલ દીવો

The evening close. The five-wick aarti is circled before the Bhagwan while the hall sings, and then the single-flame Mangal Divo. The verses for both are painted on marble panels beside the Simandhar Swami shrine here — no book needed.

Angi

આંગી

The adornment of the idol — fresh flowers, silver or gold foil, kesar work, a jewelled crown — arranged for parva days and sponsored occasions. It is why the Mulnayak looks different in different photographs of this derasar.

Before you go in

Temple etiquette

None of this is difficult, and nobody will scold a visitor who gets it wrong. But it is nicer to know.

Please do

  • Remove footwear outside the compound, and leave it on the racks at the gate.
  • Wash your hands and feet before stepping onto the temple floor.
  • Say “Namo Jinanam” or Jai Jinendra on entering, and fold your hands at the threshold.
  • Circumambulate clockwise — three pradakshina around the gabhara, keeping it on your right.
  • Cover your mouth with a clean cloth if you go near the idol to offer puja.
  • Sit for a few minutes after darshan. The stillness is the point of the building.
  • Ask the pujari if you are unsure of anything. It is the fastest way to learn the vidhi.

Please don’t

  • No leather anywhere inside — belts, wallets, watch straps, bags. This matters a great deal in a Jain temple.
  • No food or drink, and please do not come having eaten immediately before puja.
  • Nothing that has been tasted or used may be offered — offerings must be fresh and untouched.
  • No photography inside the gabhara without asking. Some derasars allow it, some do not; assume not until told otherwise.
  • No phone calls in the mandap. Silence it at the gate.
  • Do not touch the idol unless you are performing puja with the proper vidhi, bathed and in puja clothes.
  • Do not turn your back on the Bhagwan while leaving the gabhara — step back, then turn.

What to wear

Clean, modest, covered shoulders and knees. For puja proper, the tradition is unstitched or minimally stitched cotton or silk — dhoti and khes for men, sari or chaniya-choli for women — kept aside and worn only in the derasar. For darshan alone, ordinary clean clothes are perfectly fine.

What to carry

A clean handkerchief or muhpatti for covering the mouth; a small asan or cloth to sit on for chaityavandan; flowers or fruit if you wish to offer them. Kesar, sukhad, water and lamps are provided at the derasar — nothing needs buying at the gate.

If it is your first time

You do not need to know any of the sutras. Leave your shoes at the gate, wash, walk in quietly, fold your hands, go clockwise, sit for a while, and leave the same way. That is a complete visit, and it is enough.

The first thing anyone learns

Navkar Mantra

The Namokar — the mantra that bows not to any one Jina but to every soul that has reached those five states. It names no god and asks for nothing.

નમો અરિહંતાણં
નમો સિદ્ધાણં
નમો આયરિયાણં
નમો ઉવજ્ઝાયાણં
નમો લોએ સવ્વસાહૂણં

I bow to the Arihants, who have conquered and still teach.
I bow to the Siddhas, wholly liberated.
I bow to the Acharyas, who lead the order.
I bow to the Upadhyayas, who teach the scripture.
I bow to all Sadhus in the world.

Common questions

Before you set out

Can non-Jains visit the derasar?

Yes. Visitors of any faith are welcome for darshan, provided the temple’s rules are kept — no leather, no food, footwear off, and quiet inside. The restrictions that exist are about ritual purity and ahimsa, not about who you are.

Is there an entry fee?

No. Jain derasars do not charge for darshan. Offerings and seva are entirely voluntary and are made to the Sangh’s bhandar or to the trust directly.

Can I take photographs?

Please ask at the derasar. Photography of the exterior and the mandap is usually accepted; photography of the idol inside the gabhara, and photography during puja, should not be assumed. The images on this website come from the derasar’s own public Google Business Profile.

I want to sponsor a puja. How?

Speak to the Sangh directly on 099674 76524, or send the details through the seva page. Snatra puja, angi, ashtaprakari puja and the ghee for the evening lamps are all commonly sponsored, and dates for parva days go quickly.

Is the derasar accessible for elderly visitors?

Visitors have noted on Google that the main-road gate is often kept closed and that entry is from the side street, up roughly eleven steps. There is no lift. If someone in your group cannot manage steps, please call ahead so the Sangh can advise on the easiest approach on the day.

What time is the evening aarti?

Customarily around 7 p.m., which is what the schedule above shows — but it moves with the season and with the parva calendar, and on mahotsav evenings it runs much later. Confirm on the phone for the day you plan to come.
