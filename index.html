Jai Jinendra

A derasar the neighbourhood grew around

Turn off the main Karelibaug road, past Arya Kanya Vidyalaya, and the traffic simply stops. Two carved elephants stand at the steps, a sandstone frieze runs the length of the compound wall, and above it the white shikhar carries its dhwaja into the Vadodara sky.

Inside, the gabhara opens on the Mulnayak — Shri Munisuvrat Swami Bhagwan, twentieth of the twenty-four Tirthankaras, carved in shyam stone and seated in a gilded parikar. On either side are Shri Naminath Bhagwan and Shri Mahavir Swami Bhagwan, and the marble around them is worked in coloured inlay: chhatras, kalash, lotuses, and the ashtamangal repeated in quiet rows.

The derasar is cared for by Shri Karelibaug Shwetambar Murtipujak Jain Sangh, whose nameplate you pass under at the gate. Prakshal in the morning, aarti in the evening, pathshala for the children in the hall behind, and the anukampa work that goes out from here into the city — all of it continues, day after ordinary day.

Today at the derasar

From prakshal to Mangal Divo

Two darshan windows

Like most derasars, this one keeps a morning and an evening. The sanctum rests through the early afternoon after the madhyahn puja, and the doors open again for the evening chaityavandan and aarti.

Timings shift on parva days — a quick call to 099674 76524 confirms the day you are coming.

On a parva evening

Diyas are set along the whole compound wall, the Mulnayak receives an angi, and the aarti runs long. Posh Dashami, Paryushan and Mahavir Janma Kalyanak are the evenings to come early for.

The quietest hour

Early morning, while the prakshal is being done and the marble is still cool. Almost nobody is there, and the derasar is at its most itself.

In the gabhara

Three Jina, one sanctum

The pratishtha inscriptions set into the marble below the idols record the families who installed each one — from Kosamba, from Vadodara, from Dahegam. They are read every time somebody stops on the way out.

    The 20th Tirthankara — shyam-varna, lanchhan the tortoise, born at Rajgruhi. The central idol of this derasar.

What is here

The whole complex, at a glance

Shikhar & dhwaja

The curvilinear tower over the gabhara, ribbed with spirelets, crowned by amalaka and kalash, with the dhwaja changed by hand from the ladder you can see on it.

Torana gate

Rose sandstone carved with makara brackets and standing figures, under the dharmachakra, two golden lions and the Sangh’s nameplate.

Silver doors

Silver-plated gabhara doors worked with standing figures, and a hanging toran of silver ghantis along the lintel.

Inlaid floor

White marble with coloured-stone inlay — vine borders and great lotus medallions you notice only mid-chaityavandan.

The hall & pathshala

Behind the derasar, a cool marble hall hung with three framed tirth-patas of the great Jain tirths — and a blackboard on a stand, where the children of the Sangh learn their sutras.

The Jain year

Parva days worth planning around

8 days

Paryushan Mahaparva

Bhadrapad. Daily pravachan, the Kalpasutra vachan, tapasya across the Sangh, and on the last day the Barsa-sutra and the great pratikraman.

Samvatsari closes with micchami dukkadam — asked of everyone, granted to everyone.

Mahavir Janma Kalyanak

Chaitra Sud 13. The busiest day in the derasar’s year — janma-vachan, snatra puja, an evening bhavna, and a procession that fills the lane outside.

Come well before the puja if you want to be inside the mandap.

Posh Dashami

Posh Vad 10. The janma-kalyanak of Shri Parshwanath Bhagwan, kept here with attham tap, abhishek and three days of puja.

The three-day mahotsav programme goes up on the compound wall each year.
